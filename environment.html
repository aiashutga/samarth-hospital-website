How Samarth Hospital Handles Bio-Medical Waste

Recorded by colour category

Waste is recorded under four colour categories (yellow, red, white and blue), and a quantity is noted for each category in every month of 2025.

Treated at a common facility

Waste is handed to a common bio-medical waste treatment facility (CBWTF) for treatment and disposal.

Two treatment routes

Yellow-category waste (about 1,047.07 kg, 70%) is incinerable. Red, white and blue waste (about 448.74 kg, 30%) is autoclavable.

Steady volumes

Monthly totals stayed between about 112 kg (September) and 141 kg (July), about 4.1 kg a day on average for a 25-bed hospital.

Why this matters

Used syringes, sharps, dressings and expired medicines can spread infection or cause injury, and can pollute soil, water and air if mixed with ordinary rubbish. Separating and treating them properly lowers these risks for everyone.

What the Colour Categories Mean

Yellow Category Incinerable

Human anatomical waste, soiled waste such as dressings, expired or discarded medicines, chemical waste; treated by incineration.

Red Category Autoclavable

Contaminated recyclable plastic items such as tubing, IV sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles and gloves; autoclavable.

White Category Autoclavable

Waste sharps, including metals; autoclavable.

Blue Category Autoclavable

Glassware and metallic body implants; autoclavable.

General information from the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016, which define the categories in full.
